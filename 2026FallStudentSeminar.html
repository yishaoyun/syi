    Abstracts

    Jiawei Yang - Jordan induction of Arthur packet and a support equality

    In this talk, I will discuss Arthur packets for real reductive groups within the microlocal framework of Adams–Barbasch–Vogan. We first introduce the geometric parameter space, equivariant perverse sheaves, and the microlocal characterization of packet membership. We then present recent work by Adams–Ionov–Mason-Brown–Vogan on Jordan induction and the unitarity of microlocal Arthur packets. Finally, we describe a support equality theorem, which identifies these packets with the irreducible constituents obtained from suitable unipotent packets via cohomological induction followed by real normalized parabolic induction. We emphasize the interplay between coherent continuation, geometric convolution, and microlocal support, explaining how these tools control individual constituents beyond the information given by virtual character identities. This support equality is established in my recent work.

    Boyi Zheng - Dirichlet's Theorem and the Development of Modern Number Theory (1)

    This lecture explains Dirichlet's most classical proof using the class number formula. It begins with the prime number theorem and Dirichlet characters, and proceeds to the nonvanishing property of Dirichlet $L$-functions for non-real characters. The real-character case is postponed to the next lecture due to the length of the class number formula.

    Boyi Zheng - Dirichlet's Theorem and the Development of Modern Number Theory (2)

    This lecture introduces primitive Dirichlet characters and reviews key definitions and results from algebraic number theory, laying the groundwork for Dirichlet’s proof in the case of real characters.

    Boyi Zheng - Dirichlet's Theorem and the Development of Modern Number Theory (3)

    This lecture explains Dirichlet’s proof in the case of real characters.

    Longcan Lin - Projective Geometry and Its Applications

    This talk covers classical projective geometry. We first construct the extended plane by adding ideal elements (elements at infinity) to the Euclidean plane, establish the model of the projective plane, and identify $\mathbb R^2$ with the affine part of $\mathbb RP^2$. Homogeneous coordinates and the inhomogeneous parametric representation of points are then introduced. We conclude with one-dimensional fundamental projective correspondences, projective transformations, conic sections, and several famous theorems in projective geometry.

    Zimo Wang - The Prime Number Theorem and the Mean Value of the Liouville Function

    We prove, in both directions, that the prime number theorem is equivalent to \[ \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lambda(n)=0, \qquad \lambda(n)=(-1)^{\Omega(n)}. \] Here \(\Omega(n)\) counts prime factors with multiplicity. The proof uses unique factorization, Dirichlet convolution, the Dirichlet hyperbola method, and elementary estimates for real sums and integrals.

    Ziyan Wei - Finite Weyl group and Richardson varieties

    In this talk (research report), I will present my research on the relationship between finite Weyl groups and open Richardson varieties, with particular emphasis on how $R$-polynomials from algebraic combinatorics serve as a bridge between them. I will introduce the definitions and background of these objects from the perspectives of root systems, combinatorics, and geometry, discuss some of their basic properties, and highlight the interesting connections among them. Also I will introduce some cluster algebras and its application in this topic.

    Yuchi Peng - Basic Equivariant Cohomology

    Cohomology is one of the most fundamental tools for studying topological spaces. It assigns to each space a graded ring that encodes global information in an algebraically manageable form.
    Many spaces of interest in geometry and topology carry additional structure: they admit symmetries. A symmetry is formalized by the action of a group $G$ on the space. When such a group action is present, ordinary cohomology is often insufficient.
    Equivariant cohomology addresses precisely this issue. The equivariant cohomology of the original $G$-space is then defined as the ordinary cohomology of homotopy quotient. Remarkably, this construction not only resolves the problem of degenerate orbit spaces but also connects algebraic topology with differential geometry, symplectic geometry and so on.
